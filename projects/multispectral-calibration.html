The problem

Multispectral aerial images need geometric and radiometric correction, while a photogrammetry workflow must preserve their useful measurement information.

My contribution

At the Institute of Space Technology, I built/prototyped calibration steps and integrated C++ code into OpenDroneMap for an HEC-funded unmanned aerial forest-management R&D project.

What I delivered

  • Calibration stages for vignetting, dark-current correction, geometric undistortion, and radiometric calibration.
  • Processing of Green, Red, Red Edge, and NIR imagery from the Parrot Sequoia camera.
  • An OpenDroneMap workflow extension preserving 16-bit orthomosaic output, alongside Linux build and integration work.

Engineering decisions

  • Treat the camera model and radiometric corrections as part of the measurement pipeline.
  • Prototype corrections and integrate the working stages into the existing C++ photogrammetry workflow.
  • Preserve pixel depth through output rather than discarding information during conversion.

Outcome & scope

Delivered calibration and photogrammetry integration work during Aug 2019–Jun 2020, including four-band processing and 16-bit orthomosaic capability.

The registry also records approximately 12.1 seconds per image for the calibration pipeline. These are historical workflow results, not a benchmark on current hardware.

The public ODM release note corroborates the feature and timeline; it does not establish individual authorship of the entire multispectral feature set.

Skills strengthened

This was formative work: learning camera models, radiometry, C++, and photogrammetry while solving a real research problem outside my initial comfort zone.
